Alt text workshop

A screen reader can't see pictures. It reads the alt attribute out loud instead. Pick an image, describe it, and get instant feedback.

    Three kinds of images

    The right alt text depends on why the image is there.

    🖼

    Informative

    Describe the content: alt="Red-roofed house beside a tree on a sunny day"

    🔍

    Functional

    Describe the action, not the picture: a magnifying-glass button gets alt="Search".

    ✨

    Decorative

    Use alt="" so screen readers skip it. Leaving alt out makes them read the file name instead.

    What I learned

    • WCAG 1.1.1 Non-text Content: every meaningful image needs a text alternative that serves the same purpose.
    • Good alt text is short (about 125 characters), specific, and never starts with "image of".
    • Charts need their key message in the alt text, such as the trend or the highest value, not just "chart".
    • Inline SVGs get the same treatment through role="img" and a <title>.
